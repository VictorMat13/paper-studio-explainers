## What "better" means, concretely

1. **Fill the frame with big hero objects.** The main object of a shot spans at least 60% of the safe-box width (cards 760-900 px wide, the laptop about 900 px, the slab 240 px+, robots 300 px+ tall when they are the subject). Nothing small floating in the middle of empty paper. If a shot has one object, give it a stage: a paper sheet under it, a second object behind it, a shadow that anchors it.
2. **Depth in layers.** Every shot has a foreground, a subject and a background layer that move at different rates under the camera (parallax 1.0 / 0.85 / 0.7): a paper sheet or two peeking at the edges behind the subject, a chip or sprite in front catching the push. Cards get a real 3D lean (6-12 deg) and a visible thickness edge; stacks fan out; the terminal card tilts on entry and settles.
3. **Physical materials, not flat shapes.** Paper cards show a faint paper texture and a soft inner edge; the slab is matte charcoal with a highlight on its top edge; the wire casts a shadow; chips are glossy buttons with the inner highlight. Use the photoreal cut-outs big (camera, tag, eraser, scissors, laptop, robot) and let them overlap the cards they act on.
4. **The hands scene, once per body.** Victor's favourite scene is ep04's human hand vs robot hand (`HandsScene` / `Cutout kind human|robot`, hands big from the frame edges, fingertips near the centre with the wire between them). Each body uses it exactly once, at its "tell Claude" beat: BodyA A14a-A14b, BodyB B09b, BodyC C08a-C08b. The human hand brings the word chip or prompt in from the left, the robot hand holds the terminal or runs the command on the right. Hooks keep no hands.
5. **Captions with presence.** Key phrases at the top of the skill's range (bold italic 96 px, bars 66 px, text-only beats 130 px), tight tracking, always on their own paper lane (a faint white lane behind the hud text where the ground is busy), never cramped against objects. One caption line per beat, never two stacked lines competing.
6. **Every frame alive.** Ambient drift on every object (0.5-1.5 px breathing, 0.3 deg sway), sprites blink or tilt their heads, laptops' screens show a faint moving cursor, the wire draws with a travelling node highlight, paper sheets flutter 1-2 px when something lands near them. Hold, move, hold camera with a zoom on every reveal; a tracked object stays sharp while the paper streaks.
7. **Transitions that feel physical.** Objects enter by sliding from an edge with their shadow lagging two frames, or by landing with a 2-frame squash; they leave by sliding out or folding into the next card. Never a plain fade in or out on a subject. A crossing between scenes is a blur break only inside a body, and both sides are busy while they cross.

9. **Neatness.** Everything on an 8 px grid, consistent radii (14 px cards, 24 px bubbles, pill 999), consistent shadows (the skill's stack), consistent chip radii across groups (r 44, slab 240 px). The same object looks the same in every video (commit card, wire, terminal, slab, word chips, CTA).
